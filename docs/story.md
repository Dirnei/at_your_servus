# Story: Servus, Heir

The story bible for the quest line. Every quest text follows the names and beats here. Paracelsus' own story stays in Alchemy's journal; the quests only point to it.

## The people and places

- **Vinzenz Lechner** (1931 – ?), the player's great-uncle. Mining engineer at the Schwaz mines until they closed, then a tinkerer, collector and, in secret, alchemist. Writes in short, dry sentences, with a soft spot for machines and an old man's jokes. Never married. Signs every letter "Your Vinzenz".
- **The player**, his heir. Vinzenz never met them as a grown-up; he addresses them as "heir" or "young one", never by name or gender.
- **The Lechnerhof**, the estate in the hills above Schwaz in Tyrol: a farmhouse, a run-down works with a water wheel and a steam engine, a barn, and a cellar cut into the rock.
- **Lechner & Compagnie**, the family's trading house, founded in 1521. Its factors traded Schwaz silver to Augsburg and Venice. Long gone; Vinzenz wanted to bring it back.
- **The cellar**: where Vinzenz kept Paracelsus' Journal. Family lore says Paracelsus stayed with the Lechners in Schwaz in 1533 and left the journal behind.
- **The old gnome**: the Old Miner from Alchemy's journal, Paracelsus' friend who stayed below Schwaz. Vinzenz found him.

## How the letters work

Vinzenz left a bundle of sealed letters with the notary, each marked "to be opened when ...". Every quest text is one of these letters, a note pinned to a machine, or a page from his workbook. Letters open with "Servus, heir," or "Servus, young one,". "Servus" means both hello and goodbye in Tyrol, and the last letter uses it as goodbye.

## The mystery

Vinzenz disappeared two winters ago. No body, no note. The letters reveal step by step what happened:

1. He finished the Great Work: the red Athanor burned in his cellar.
2. The Stone could make everything that is. He wanted to make what is not yet, "beyond the Stone".
3. He followed the old gnome's knocking deep into the mountain and did not come back.
4. He is not dead. He stayed below with the old gnome, tending the veins of the mountain, as Paracelsus' gnome did for five hundred years. The finale's last letter, found "where dust trickles from one block", tells the heir so and says goodbye.

## The last letter (finale)

> Servus, young one,
>
> If you read this, you have made what I could only draw. Good. I knew the house would be in better hands.
>
> I did not die. I went below, after the knocking. The old one was waiting, the same one who waited for Paracelsus. There is work here that no Stone does: the veins must be tended, and the mountain must be listened to. I am slow now and very happy.
>
> Do not come looking. Leave a pinch of salt in the deepslate now and then, and listen. If it knocks twice, that is me.
>
> The trading house is yours. Keep the books straight.
>
> Servus,
> Your Vinzenz
